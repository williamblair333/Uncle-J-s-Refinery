# media-teardown

Content that cites a source is two layers: what the source said, and what the creator
built on it. Judge them separately. A real source under a sales pitch is common, and
"the video is a funnel" is not the same finding as "the claims are false".

## Output — these parts, in this order

1. **Verdict.** One or two sentences: is it worth their time, and what it actually is.
2. **Source vs spin.** For each cited source (person, study, post): what the source
   actually said, then what the creator added, stretched, or reframed. If you can look the
   source up, do, and cite it. If you can't, say "source not checked".
3. **Claims.** A table, one row per factual claim:

   | Claim | Status | Note |

   Status is exactly one of: **verified** (you checked a source), **matches source**
   (true to what was cited, source itself unproven), **unverified** (no source given or
   none checked), **false** (you have contrary evidence; cite it), **opinion** (a judgment,
   not checkable). A claim with no source is *unverified*, not false. Say "made up" only
   with evidence.
4. **Omissions.** What a fair version would have said and this one didn't. Examples:
   estimates presented as data, numbers that don't add up, the creator's own stake or
   affiliation, the price, a limitation of the tool or advice, a counterexample. At least
   one line. If you find none, write "None found" and name what you checked.
5. **Motive.** Ask "why?" of what the content does until you reach a value (revenue,
   traffic, status), one step per line. Name the call to action and what it costs, if
   that's knowable.
6. **Keep.** The ideas worth keeping, each with the condition under which it holds.
   Note anything that contradicts the content's own good points.

Cite every number you add yourself, or label it "estimate". Keep the whole thing under
about 500 words unless the user asks for more.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the source as unverifiable and stopping there, instead of separating the
  source's claim from the creator's extrapolation ("the gap grows every month").
- Calling an unsourced claim false. Missing evidence makes it *unverified*.
- Skipping omissions because nothing jumped out. They are the part readers can't see,
  so the slot is required.
- Judging the advice against the user's own setup before laying out what the content says.
  Do the teardown first, then relate it to them in **Keep**.
