# Feynman Style

Write so a smart reader new to the subject can follow without losing accuracy. Apply this to any subject, format, or audience.

## Sentences and words

- Use short sentences and common words.
- Keep one main idea in each sentence.
- Prefer active voice. Use passive voice only when the actor does not matter.
- Prefer concrete verbs: "measure", not "perform a measurement of".
- Name the specific thing. Avoid vague words such as "aspect", "factor", and "solution" unless the meaning is exact.
- Keep terms consistent. Do not swap words for variety.
- Use a neutral tone. Describe rather than persuade unless persuasion is the task.

## Explaining ideas

- Start from what the reader already knows.
- State the main point before the supporting detail.
- Explain why something matters or works, not only what it is.
- Use a concrete example when an idea is abstract.
- Separate facts, inferences, and opinions.
- State important limits and uncertainty.

## Technical terms

- Use a technical term only when it is the precise word.
- Define it once, in plain words, at first use.
- Do not coin a term when a plain phrase will do.
- Spell out an uncommon abbreviation at first use.

## Remove

- Throat-clearing: "it is worth noting that", "it is important to emphasize".
- Inflated phrases: "in order to", "due to the fact that", "a large number of".
- Unearned boosters: "novel", "robust", "groundbreaking", "world-class".
- Empty hedges. Keep a hedge only when it reports real uncertainty.
- Any sentence that can be deleted without losing meaning.

## Match the format

- Follow any required structure, template, style guide, or legal wording.
- Keep the details the format depends on, such as equations, code, citations, data, dates, and conditions.
- Do not simplify exact terms when the change would alter meaning.
- Never invent facts, data, sources, or quotations. Mark missing material as `[TODO: ...]`.
- Do not state more certainty than the evidence supports.
- Match formality to the setting. Plain does not mean casual.

## Final check

1. Read each sentence aloud. Rewrite any that are hard to say.
2. Replace every word the intended reader may not know, unless it is defined and necessary.
3. Confirm simplification did not change a claim, number, or condition.
4. Confirm each paragraph makes one clear point.
